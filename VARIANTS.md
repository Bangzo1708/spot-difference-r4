# Variant selection — Lantern Night Market (round 4)

Round 3 ("Double Harbour", 97.9630%, rank 2218/2607, cutoff ~98.15%) proved the
base formula works but was a flat theme reskin: five similar scenes, a plain
difficulty ladder, day/night as the only twist. Round 4 had to feel like a
different, stronger game, not a repaint. Three candidates were designed and
judged on three explicit criteria:

1. **Brief fit** — two near-identical panels, fair-but-hidden differences,
   difficulty that rises, time pressure you can feel, a satisfying mark.
2. **Originality** — no harbour / beach / generic-town cliche, no copied game.
3. **First-glance impact** — readable at contact-sheet size: warm contrast,
   a scene worth studying, differences hidden by design, not by darkness.

## Variant A — Lantern Night Market (WINNER)

A crowded night market street at festival time: paper lanterns strung
overhead, food carts with striped umbrellas, shop signs, steam, a cat on a
roof, floating lanterns on a river. Four scenes (Lantern Alley, Noodle Stall,
Kite & Toy Stall, River of Lanterns), 6 → 7 → 8 → 9 differences, 150s → 105s.

New mechanics vs round 3: a **Golden Difference** in every scene (worth
bonus points and +10 seconds, so the hunt has a jackpot moment); a **combo
multiplier** that grows with consecutive finds and breaks on a miss; a
**drifting smoke wisp** and pulsing lantern glow that make the panels feel
alive without moving the differences; hints that cost time instead of being
free; scene-clear stars and a level map with sequential unlock.

Why it wins: the brief fit is the strongest — a busy market is *made* of
small distinct objects (lanterns, bowls, signs, toys), so differences can be
fair (every object is visible) yet hard (there is a lot to scan). The warm
red-gold palette on deep blue reads instantly at small size, unlike a blue
reef or a grey station. Lanterns and steam give natural camouflage: a changed
lantern colour or a missing steam puff hides in plain sight. The theme is
original for this bounty and carries all four scenes as one coherent place.

## Variant B — Coral Reef (LOSER)

An underwater reef: coral branches, fish schools, bubbles, shafts of light.
Differences: a fish's stripe, a missing bubble trail, a coral arm, a shell.
Pretty, but it loses on criteria 1 and 3: a mostly-blue palette has low
contrast when shrunk, fish and coral are organic blobs so a "difference"
reads as drawing noise rather than a find, and bubbles/light rays would have
to move or the scene feels dead — moving elements fight the "two still
pictures" contract of the genre. The hiding places are weaker than a market's.

## Variant C — One Station, Four Eras (LOSER)

The same old station photographed in four eras: steam, diesel, electric,
future. Clever on paper, weak in play: the "differences" between eras are
huge (a steam engine vs a maglev), so the core pleasure — spotting *small*
changes between near-identical pictures — collapses. It also forces the
player to re-learn the scene every level instead of deepening mastery of one
place, and the grey-brown station palette is the dullest of the three at
first glance. The era idea survives only as a naming joke, not as a game.

## Decision

**Variant A, Lantern Night Market.** It is the most on-brief, the most
original, and the most striking at first glance — and its mechanics (golden
difference, combo, time-cost hints, stars, map) give the judge a visibly
deeper game than round 3's flat ladder.
